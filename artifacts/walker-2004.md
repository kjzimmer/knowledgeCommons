## Summary

Defines three related attributes of social–ecological systems. **Resilience** is the capacity of a system to
absorb disturbance and reorganize while keeping essentially the same function, structure, identity and feedbacks.
**Adaptability** is the collective capacity of the people in the system to manage its resilience.
**Transformability** is the capacity to create a fundamentally new system when ecological, economic or social
conditions make the existing one untenable. Resilience has four aspects: latitude (how far the system can be
changed before it loses the ability to recover), resistance (how hard it is to change), precariousness (how close
it currently is to a threshold), and panarchy (how these are shaped by systems at scales above and below).
The paper stresses that resilience is not always desirable: a system can be resilient in an undesirable state.
