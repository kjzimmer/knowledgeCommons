## Summary

Examines large state schemes, among them scientific forestry, planned cities such as Brasília, Soviet
collectivization and compulsory villagization in Tanzania, that simplified complex local realities to make them
"legible" and controllable. Scott argues they failed when four things combined: administrative simplification, a
"high-modernist" faith in planning, an authoritarian state willing to impose it, and a civil society unable to
resist. He contrasts formal knowledge with *mētis*, practical knowledge rooted in local experience. Scott is
explicit that not all state simplification is harmful.
