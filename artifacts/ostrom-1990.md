## Summary

Challenges the view that common-pool resources must be either privatized or run by the state. Drawing on case
studies such as Swiss and Japanese mountain commons, Spanish *huerta* irrigation systems and Philippine *zanjera*
irrigation communities, Ostrom shows that resource users have often built lasting institutions to govern shared
resources themselves. She identifies eight design principles common to long-enduring commons, including clear
boundaries, rules fitted to local conditions, participation in making rules, monitoring, graduated sanctions,
low-cost conflict resolution, recognition of the right to organize, and nested enterprises for larger systems.
She also documents failures.
