## Summary

Argues that national defence is not the pure public good that standard economics assumes, and that many dispersed
actors (private firms, communities, local governments and individuals) already supply parts of it. The authors
explore polycentric arrangements for defence. Their argument comes from a free-market tradition and is contested.
