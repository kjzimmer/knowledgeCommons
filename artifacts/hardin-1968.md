## Summary

Argues, through the image of herders each adding animals to a shared pasture, that freedom in a commons brings ruin
to all: each user gains the whole benefit of extra use while the cost is shared. Hardin's central concern was
population growth; he called for "mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon". Critics, Ostrom most prominently, later
argued that he described open-access resources with no rules, not commons governed by communities.
