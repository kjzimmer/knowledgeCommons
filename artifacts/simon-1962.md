## Summary

Argues that complex systems, natural and social, tend to be hierarchic: made of subsystems that are themselves made
of subsystems. Such systems are "nearly decomposable": interactions within subsystems are much stronger than
interactions between them, so parts can be studied somewhat independently in the short run. Includes the parable of
two watchmakers, Hora and Tempus, to show why systems built from stable intermediate forms evolve far faster.
