---
id: leverage-points-order
claim: Changing a system's goals, its underlying paradigm or the power to transcend paradigms has far more effect on its behaviour than changing its parameters, such as taxes, subsidies or standards.
evidence:
  level: speculation
  kinds: [practitioner, theory]
  why: Meadows drew the ordering from decades of system-dynamics modelling and practice, and she called it tentative. No systematic test of the ordering exists in the commons.
agreement:
  status: unknown
  why: Widely cited and influential, but the ordering has not been tested, and its author described it as provisional.
  wouldChange: Comparative studies of interventions at different leverage points with measured effects.
objection: Parameter changes, such as carbon prices or interest rates, have reshaped whole systems; and attempts to change paradigms directly are hard to target and evaluate, so the most "powerful" points may be the least usable.
unknowns: Whether the ordering holds across domains, and how leverage points interact.
