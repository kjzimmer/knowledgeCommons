---
id: more-is-different
claim: Knowing the laws that govern a system's parts does not by itself let you reconstruct how the whole behaves; new properties appear at each level of organization.
evidence:
  level: established
  kinds: [theory, observation]
  why: Grounded in condensed-matter physics (broken symmetry, phase transitions), where the higher-level behaviour is well documented and cannot be practically derived from the lower-level laws.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: Widely accepted as a practical point about explanation. Whether emergence is only a limit of our knowledge or a feature of the world is still argued.
  wouldChange: A general method that derives higher-level behaviour from lower-level laws in the cases Anderson discusses.
objection: Strong reductionists reply that emergence is epistemic, not ontological. The higher level is fully fixed by the lower one; we just lack the computing power or concepts to derive it. On that view "more is different" describes our limits, not the world.
unknowns: Whether there are emergent properties that could not be derived even in principle.
