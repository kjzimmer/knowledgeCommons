id: resilience-definition
claim: The resilience of a social–ecological system is its capacity to absorb disturbance and reorganize while keeping essentially the same function, structure, identity and feedbacks, and it has four aspects. Latitude is how far the system can change before it cannot recover; resistance is how hard it is to change; precariousness is how close it is to a threshold; panarchy is how systems above and below shape the other three.
evidence:
  level: established
  kinds: [theory]
  why: A definitional and conceptual claim from a widely cited framework paper. "Established" here means established as a framework in use, not as an empirical finding.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: The definition is the standard one in resilience science. Measuring the four aspects in real systems is much harder and less agreed.
  wouldChange: A rival definition becoming standard, or evidence that the four aspects cannot be measured in practice.
objection: The concept has been criticized as too broad to measure and too easy to stretch, so that "resilience" can be claimed for almost any policy (suggested by the drafter, unsourced in the commons).
unknowns: How to measure latitude and precariousness before a threshold is crossed.
