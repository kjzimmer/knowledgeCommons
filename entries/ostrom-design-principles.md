id: ostrom-design-principles
claim: Long-enduring commons tend to share eight design principles, including clear boundaries, rules fitted to local conditions, participation in rule-making, monitoring, graduated sanctions, low-cost conflict resolution, recognition of the right to organize, and nested enterprises.
evidence:
  level: established
  kinds: [historical-case, analysis]
  why: Derived from Ostrom's comparative cases. A later review of studies (Cox, Arnold and Villamayor Tomás, 2010) is reported to have found broad support; that review is not yet in the commons.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: Broadly supported; the principles are correlations with endurance, not proven causes, and later work refined several of them.
  wouldChange: Systematic evidence that commons with these features fail as often as those without.
objection: The principles describe what surviving commons look like, so they may confuse features of success with causes; and they say little about large-scale commons.
unknowns: Which principles matter most, and whether they apply to knowledge commons such as this one.
