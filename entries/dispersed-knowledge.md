id: dispersed-knowledge
claim: The information needed to run a complex system is spread among the people in it. No center can gather it all.
evidence:
  level: extension
  kinds: [theory, historical-case]
  why: Hayek's argument is theoretical; the record of centrally planned economies is often cited in support, but the cases have many confounders.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: The core point about dispersed, local knowledge is widely accepted across economics. How far it limits planning, especially with modern computing, is contested.
  wouldChange: A planning system that demonstrably gathers and uses dispersed local knowledge at scale as well as prices do.
objection: Large firms, supply chains and platforms plan internally at enormous scale using data, and advocates of computational planning argue modern information technology removes Hayek's constraint.
unknowns: Whether data and computation now change the limits Hayek described.
