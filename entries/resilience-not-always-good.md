---
id: resilience-not-always-good
claim: Resilience is not always desirable. A system can be resilient in an undesirable state, and then the task is to reduce its resilience and help it move to a better one.
evidence:
  level: extension
  kinds: [theory, historical-case]
  why: Stated explicitly by the framework's authors and illustrated with ecological examples (such as degraded rangelands and lakes locked in a turbid state), but the social applications are argued rather than tested.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: Accepted within resilience science; often forgotten in popular use, where resilience is treated as simply good.
  wouldChange: Evidence that deliberately reducing a system's resilience reliably produces worse outcomes than holding it in place.
objection: Deliberately weakening a system's resilience risks collapse rather than transformation; the intended "better basin" may not be reachable, and those who bear the cost are rarely the ones choosing.
unknowns: How to tell whether a system is in an undesirable but resilient state, and how to reduce resilience without causing collapse.
