claim: Communities can govern shared resources well, under the right conditions, without central direction or privatization.
evidence:
  level: established
  kinds: [historical-case, observation]
  why: Many documented cases of long-lasting community-governed commons, in Ostrom's work and the large body of research that followed.
agreement:
  status: settled
  why: That self-governance is possible is no longer seriously disputed; Ostrom's work received the 2009 Nobel memorial prize in economics. What remains debated is under which conditions it works.
  wouldChange: Evidence that the documented cases were not in fact self-governed, or did not last.
objection: Successful cases tend to be small, local and stable; whether self-governance scales to large, global or fast-changing resources is unclear.
unknowns: How far the conditions for success carry over to large-scale and global commons.
