---
id: stability-erodes-resilience
claim: Managing a living system for stability and maximum steady yield can erode its resilience, leaving it prone to sudden collapse.
evidence:
  level: established
  kinds: [observation, historical-case, theory]
  why: Supported by ecological cases such as fisheries, forests and rangelands managed for steady yield, and by later resilience research building on Holling.
agreement:
  status: leaning
  why: Broadly accepted in ecology and natural-resource management. The mechanisms and how far the idea carries over to economies and organizations are debated.
  wouldChange: Evidence that stability-oriented management does not reduce resilience in the cases cited, or that collapses had other causes.
objection: Many collapses blamed on stability-oriented management had more direct causes, such as overharvesting driven by economic pressure or poor data, rather than the pursuit of stability as such.
unknowns: How far the pattern applies to social and economic systems, such as financial regulation aimed at smoothing cycles.
